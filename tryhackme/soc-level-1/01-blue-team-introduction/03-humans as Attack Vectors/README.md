# Humans as Attack Vectors

## Overview

This room explores why humans are considered one of the weakest security layers in an organization and how threat actors exploit human psychology through social engineering techniques.

It also introduces the role of the SOC in detecting, investigating, and helping prevent attacks that target employees.

## Key Concepts

### Human Element

People often become the easiest entry point into an organization because attackers exploit trust, curiosity, fear, and urgency rather than technical vulnerabilities.

---

### Social Engineering

Social engineering is the manipulation of individuals into performing actions that benefit an attacker.

Successful attacks generally rely on two factors:

- Trust
- Emotional manipulation

---

### Common Attacks Against Humans

#### Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to steal credentials or deliver malware through fake websites or malicious attachments.

#### Malware Downloads

Attackers trick victims into downloading malicious software through fake software, malicious QR codes, SEO poisoning, or fake CAPTCHA pages.

#### Deepfakes

AI-generated audio or video used to impersonate trusted individuals and manipulate victims into performing sensitive actions.

#### Impersonation

Attackers pretend to be trusted people, such as IT staff or executives, to obtain credentials or convince victims to perform risky actions.

#### Other Social Engineering Attacks

Examples include:

- USB drop attacks
- Insider threats
- Fake job offers
- Physical social engineering

---

## SOC Perspective

The SOC focuses on detecting attacks that bypass preventive controls and investigating suspicious user activity.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Investigating phishing alerts
- Detecting malware infections
- Supporting incident response
- Recommending security improvements

---

## Mitigation Strategies

Common defensive measures include:

- Anti-phishing solutions
- Antivirus / EDR deployment
- Security awareness training
- Phishing simulations
- "Trust but verify" principle

## Analyst Notes

### Key Takeaways

- Humans are often the easiest target for attackers.
- Social engineering exploits psychology rather than software flaws.
- Prevention and detection must work together.
